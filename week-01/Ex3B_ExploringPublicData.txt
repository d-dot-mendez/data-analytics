1. Start by reviewing the data.gov home page. What is this? 
	This is a public database that the US government created so that people can use this as a resource to conduct research, develop web and mobile apps, and more.



2. Go to Most Viewed Datasets. How many datasets are listed?
	There are 584,003 datasets available and if you go to "Most Viewed Datasets" 20 are listed, unless you click "show more results" where they will show you twenty more datasets 	listed. 

 
3. Find the Topic Category for Human Health. Review the range of data sources
available for this topic. Why do you think these were tagged with the Human Health
category?
	I noticed that many of the data sources that were available under "Human Health" were provided by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. Despite its name, its mission is 	actually to protect human health in addition to the environment. Because of this, many of the data sources mentioned something about drinking water. I clicked on one of the data 	sources that mentioned drinking water and it was talking about limiting the contaminants in drinking water. Water is essential to human life, so if three is a lethal amount of 	contaminants in the drinking water, human life would cease to remain (and this applies to the other organisms on Earth as well who relies on water).	




4. Go to the Food Price Outlook. Review the catalog page for this dataset. How can you
navigate to additional datasets from the USDA?
	There's only one source that shows up when you look for "Food Price Outlook". If you want to find additional resources you can download the file that is attached to this dataset. 	That file offers a list of web pages and their links. You can also look under the "search by tags" feature and select a few tags that are similar to what you are searching for. 	There is also a link under "Access & Use"that could possibly take you to other datasets.

8. Go back to the results for food composition, and click on FoodData Central. Review
the catalog page for this data source, then click on the button for Visit Page. Take
some time to explore the FoodData Central website
a) Try searching for butter, or any of your favorite foods. What kind of results do you
get? What happens when you click on a result?
	When I searched butter, I saw a table chart at the bottom of the page. In that table chart was the NDB number, a list of different types of butter, the most recent acquisition date and the SR/Foundation Food Category. When I clicked on "Butter stick, unsalted" I was taken to a page with an even larger table chart that shows the proximates, minerals, and lipids that the unsalted butter stick is composed of. 


b) Go back to the home page. Find Foundation Foods, and click on Downloads. What
kind of data can you find on this page? How big are these files? Where can you find
more information on the data types available?
	On this page, you can find supporting data, data on Foundation Foods, FNDDS, Branded, SR Legacy, and you can find full downloads of all data types. These files vary in size but 	some of them are 54M, 460M when unzipped and other are 66.8M,663M, and 709M when zipped. You can find more information on the data types available under resources. There they have 	a list of other databases, documents, and tools that you can use.
